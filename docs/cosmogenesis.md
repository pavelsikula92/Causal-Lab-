# Cosmogenesis: how the axioms read the birth of spacetime and matter

Written on 9 Nov 2026 from the discussion with the author. **Status: an interpretation and a chain of hypotheses, not a
result.** Each statement is marked: *from the axioms* (follows from Part V of the paper), *open* (the axioms do not
decide it), *new* (a hypothesis of the author, consistent with the axioms, not yet tested).

## 1. The author's picture (9 Nov)

The birth of our spacetime is an explosion into non-space: an unbounded potential for geometry to spread. The
substrate (the vacuum) collapses; this collapse stabilises the defects that arise in it (matter) and compresses the
causal influence around them. The only thing that can be measured is its resistance to the pulse.

Two remarks of the author that frame it:
- A cluster found in noise is not a precursor of matter: in an enormous amount of noise a cluster is always found, and
  none has a sharp boundary (the laboratory's own results). A sharp boundary needs a topological invariant; the
  charge of a hedgehog is an integer and cannot smear out.
- The hedgehog of F3.13 is put in by hand (a crutch); what is missing is how it forms.

## 2. Scales

- A million events in 3+1 fill a region only ~10⁶^(1/4) ≈ 32 discreteness lengths across; with ℓ near the Planck length
  that is of order 10⁻⁴² s.
- A minute is ~10⁴⁵ discreteness times; the causal past of an observer at that age holds of order 10¹⁸⁰ events.
- The first million events are the very beginning; by the first minute the universe is, on this scale, old.

## 3. The phases as the axioms read them

**Phase 0 — the first event.** *From the axioms:* reality consists of events, acts of realisation without position,
duration or energy (Axiom of the primacy of events); time is the order itself, with no external time (Axiom of the
causal order). There is no "before" the first event, not even empty time. *Open:* whether there is a single first
event (the causal nucleation of the first version) and what starts it; the growth law is open (Axiom of the dynamics of
the order).

**Phase 1 — ~1 to 10³ events: order without geometry.** *From the axioms:* every new event arises in the future of some
existing ones; there is "before" and "after", and longest chains already count proper time; there is no dimension, no
distance, no space (the Axiom of the substrate promises them only on the plateau). *Open:* where the order goes. Most
partial orders are three-layer structures without any geometry (Kleitman–Rothschild); local growth with bounded degree
gives no manifold (F6.1, F6.2). Whether spacetime grows out of the order is decided by the weighting with the action of
the order (Benincasa–Dowker), which the axiom asserts and nobody has shown in 4D.

**Phase 2 — ~10³ to 10⁶ events: the plateau is born.** *From the axioms and the laboratory:* if the growth leads to a
manifold-like order, geometry becomes readable blindly from a size of about N* = 32 000 events in 3+1 (K.1). Around a
million events it first makes sense to speak of a four-dimensional spacetime without a preferred frame.
*Derived consequence:* **matter cannot arise before geometry.** A flow carries a four-velocity u^a (Axiom of momentum),
a timelike direction, which can be read from the order only on the plateau. Before the plateau there is no direction
of motion, hence no inertia and no matter. The order of emergence is fixed: order → geometry → flow with momentum →
matter.

**Phase 3 — flow and saturation: the hot phase.** *From the axioms:* influence spreads along the relations and is
conserved (Axiom of continuity); each event passes only a bounded flow (Axiom of finite capacity). In a small, densely
connected early order through which the initial influence passes (the author's causal pulse) the flow meets the
capacity everywhere; the excess is stored (ΔQ) and almost all events are saturated: a state without structure, equally
"hot" everywhere, with disordered directions. *Open:* where the initial influence comes from (the source term s_v of
the continuity axiom is not specified for the early universe).

**Phase 4 — cooling and the birth of matter.** *From the axioms:* as the order grows, the volume over which the
influence spreads grows and the saturation falls: the order cools. *From physics in general (Kibble 1976, Zurek 1985):*
when a system is cooled quickly through a transition where directions should align, it cannot align everywhere alike;
regions that know nothing of each other causally choose different directions, and topological defects with a sharp
boundary form at their borders by themselves, with a density set by the rate of cooling (confirmed in superfluid helium,
superconductors and ion traps). Kibble proposed the mechanism precisely for the early universe. *Link to the
laboratory:* F3.13d (spontaneous defects, FAILED) tried a cold flow at rest, without a transition and without cooling,
which is the situation in which the Kibble–Zurek mechanism predicts no defects. The early universe is the textbook case
of a fast quench.

**Phase 5 — up to the first minute.** *From the axioms:* on the plateau geometry holds, and through the completion by
general relativity (adopted, not derived) the universe expands as in standard cosmology; around the first minute the
light nuclei form as in the standard model. *Own content of the theory:* discreteness (the age term, fluctuations of
Λ). The big questions of cosmology (why the universe is so homogeneous, where the seeds of galaxies come from) are not
addressed by the axioms. Sorkin's "cosmological renormalisation" (cycles of growth that reset the universe) is an idea
from causal set theory, not part of the theory.

In this picture matter and gravity arise from one process: a defect is where the relaxation got stuck, and the
compression of the order around it is its gravitational field.

## 5. What it requires and how it can fail

- **The monopole problem.** The Kibble mechanism produces defects at a density set by the rate of cooling, and in
  classical cosmology it produces far too many (the reason inflation was proposed). If matter is made of defects, the
  theory must one day compute how many form and match the observed density of matter. That is a real numerical
  prediction, not a weakness.
- **Sharp boundary.** Only a topological charge gives one; density clusters do not (section 1).
- **Order of emergence.** No matter before the plateau (phase 2); a test that forms matter in a pre-geometric order
  contradicts the axioms.
