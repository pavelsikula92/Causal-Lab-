# Hypotheses (ideas, not tests)

Ideas written down so that they are not lost. An entry here claims nothing: it says what the idea is, what the
laboratory already knows about it, and what would test it. A hypothesis becomes a test only through a card
(pre-registered question, instruments, criteria), like every other test.

## H1. The age length L_* as the scale of matter (author, 24 Nov 2026)

**Idea.** The age term of a static field becomes of order one at the length L_* = (c_T ℓ_k² T)^{1/3} (paper, Part
"Predictions", eq. Lstar; c_T = 0.1035, the conformal sector). The geometry read from the order differs below and above
L_*. The author's earlier idea of a saturation of the light cone as materialisation (refuted on the substrate: the
saturation series F3.3, feedback removed the saturation every time) may return here: what happens at L_* could be where
a field localises.

**What is known.**
- For ℓ_k equal to the Planck length and T the age of the universe, L_* = 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁵ m (the table of the paper); the
  proton charge radius is 0.84 × 10⁻¹⁵ m. **A coincidence within a factor 2, nothing more yet:** it holds for one
  reading of T, it uses the conformal age term only (the optical age term of old fields is open, F6.8i), and at L_* the
  expansion in the age term breaks down.
- At L_* lies the static pole of the spin-0 channel (P3 of the paper): K̂ = 0. Whether it is a standing wave, an
  instability of the static sector or only the limit of the expansion is open.
- Decision 18: what a particle is belongs to the quantum part, built on the classical base. H1 is a question for that
  part.

**What would test it.**
1. F6.8i first: whether an old field has a finite static response, and with it whether L_* survives the optical
   sector.
2. The static spin-0 channel at K̂ → 0 (P3): the static response beyond the pole, at fixed age, from the expected BD
   action (no age expansion). If the static sector is unstable there, the length at which it happens and the shape of
   the unstable mode are the first numbers H1 needs.
3. Only then a comparison with hadronic scales, with T fixed by a theory of the age (F8.8), not chosen to fit.

## H2. The age term as the scale dependence of geometry (author, 24 Nov 2026)

**Idea.** The age term joins scale and time: δK̂/K̂ ≃ −0.10 k³ℓ_k² T. The same order looks different depending on how
large a piece of spacetime and how long a field is observed; the geometry between protons and between galaxies need not
be the same. The author's picture: young fields are pliant, old ones change their texture.

**What is known (and one correction of the picture).**
- With age the static stiffness **falls**, it does not harden: K̂ decreases linearly in T in the conformal and mixed
  sectors (F6.8a, F6.8b2), and in the optical sector it falls as (kT)⁵ at second order (F6.8e3) and faster at finite
  amplitude (F6.8f); with the local part neglected it would change sign near kT ≃ 45 at k ℓ_k = 0.13.
- The "roughening" holds in another sense: with age light focuses within the causal intervals (ε = b(kT)² ≥ π²),
  caustics, several rays and cut loci appear; the causal geometry of an old field is folded. Old fields are softer and
  folded, not harder.
- Whether the softening stops (a static limit, saturation of the age term) is exactly the question of F6.8i.

**What would test it.**
- F6.8i (the old-field static response); F6.11 (the age term in the full static propagator); F8.8 (the meaning of T;
  fields of different ages at the same scale).

## H3. The age term as a scaler that the classical theory cannot close (author and Claude, 27 Nov 2026)

**Idea.** The age term joins the discreteness length ℓ_k, the age T and the amplitude b of a field into new scales
(H1, H2). At a cusp of the first arrival it does so with a non-integer power. The term then does not vanish in the
continuum limit ℓ_k → 0. Something beyond the classical expected action must regulate it, and the author expects that
to be the quantum level of causal physics, where matter and particles belong (decision 18). The scale at which the
regulation acts would be a candidate scale of matter.

**What is known.**
- **The cusp term.** Around each cusp of the first arrival (π² < ε < 4π², one cusp per half-plane p ≥ 0, multiplicity 1),
  the age integral has the term C Ψ(ε)(2π)⁻¹ γ^{11/7}, C = −8.8324e-3, beside the smooth Φ(ε)·16γ³ (f68i_dev §6.2,
  §7.1, §7.2).
  - The exact needle reproduces the exponent at ε = 12 to 0.3 %. With C not fitted, it reproduces the coefficient to
    0.2 % at the smallest γ (record diag/out/f68i_d2d_20261127T005045Z).
  - The exponent comes from the cusp normal form (σ ~ γ^{4/7}, τ ~ γ^{6/7}); it is a catastrophe exponent, not a
    power of a Taylor series.
- **Normalisation.**
  - γ = k²/(2l₂) with k in units of ℓ_k and l₂ = V_flat(τ = 1)^{1/2} = 0.3618 (ερ = 1). So γ = 1.382 (kℓ_k)².
  - The cusp term exceeds the smooth one by (C/(32π)) γ^{−10/7} Ψ/Φ ∝ (kℓ_k)^{−20/7} (§7.1).
  - The smooth part shifts the stiffness, relative to the Einstein–Hilbert value, by ∝ (kℓ_k)² at fixed n = kT and ε
    (second order: c₅(kℓ_k)²(kT)⁵, F6.8e3; finite ε: R(ε), f68f_dev §9).
  - Hence the cusp part shifts it by ∝ (kℓ_k)^{−6/7} at fixed n and ε. **It grows as ℓ_k → 0**: the expected BD action
    of an old field (ε > π²) has no continuum limit for the static response.
  - At fixed T and b, the k-dependence is k^{29/7} f(bk²T²) with f known only on π² < ε < 4π².
- **Thin needles.** Every age term comes from causal intervals of length ~T and proper time of a few ℓ_k (s = l₂σ² of
  order 1 under the kernel). These are the intervals with the fewest elements, where the expected action is furthest
  from any single sprinkling.
- **Classical perturbations do not remove it.** A cusp is a structurally stable catastrophe of a two-parameter family
  of rays (here the direction p, θ). A generic field, not only the plane wave, has cusps with the same normal form, so
  the exponent 11/7 is generic.
- **Real fields are outside the computed range.** For them ε ≫ 4π² (Earth ~1e28). Ψ beyond the first cusp pair is not
  computed. Literal numbers would be absurdly large at any scale, as already for the second-order optical age term
  (substrate_and_action_audit §7).

**What would decide it (in this order).**
1. **The weight of §7.1 by computation.** Compare a brute-force finite-κ computation at one ε in the cusp range with
   smooth part plus cusp part (only the derivation exists now).
2. **What survives in a single order.** The cusp term from the actual BD action of sprinkled needles of a few elements,
   not from its expectation. If the fluctuations at σ ~ ℓ_k already cut it off, the regulating scale is classical.
3. **The quantum level.** In a wave description (amplitudes over chains, Johnston's propagator; Sorkin's quantum
   measure), caustics and cusps are smoothed over a width set by the wavelength and ℓ_k, as in wave catastrophes
   (Pearcey integral, Berry's indices). The questions:
   - Does the γ^{11/7} term then become a smooth term with its own scale L_c(ℓ_k, T, b)?
   - Is L_c related to L_* of H1?
   - A comparison with hadronic scales only with T fixed by a theory of the age (F8.8), not chosen to fit.

**A candidate regulator from the literature (added 28 Nov 2026, the author: "Můžem to zkusit").**
- **The paper.** S. Ramos et al. (CPT, Aix-Marseille), Phys. Rev. D, online 2 Nov 2026, DOI 10.1103/v7p6-qcq3. Popular
  account: osel.cz, 26 Nov 2026.
  - If gravity is quantum, a photon crossing a bath of gravitons meets a superposition of geometries. Its arrival time,
    and with it the edge of the light cone, is blurred by an amount growing as the square root of the time, like a
    random walk.
  - Causality is not violated.
  - Near a black hole, the blur of the horizon could reach its radius in about the Page time. That part is
    speculative by the paper's own account.
- **The connection with H3.**
  - The cusp term γ^{11/7} comes from a caustic of the light cone: the first-arrival wavefront folds where the rays
    merge (f68i_dev §6–7).
  - The blur is not itself a caustic; it is a fluctuation of the arrival time. A caustic is where a sharp front is
    most singular, so a blur of width w smooths it, as a wave smooths a classical caustic.
  - Moreover, fluctuating media form new caustics by random focusing. Both effects meet at the cusps.
- **A scale.** A blur growing as √(ℓT) would set the scale L_c of H3 from the discreteness ℓ and the age T. For ℓ the
  Planck length and T the age of the universe, √(ℓT) ≈ 5·10⁻⁵ m. This is only an illustration of the order: the
  coefficient of the paper is not known here, and ℓ need not be the Planck length.
- **The classical counterpart already in the laboratory.** F3.14c ("swerves", replicated): a defect on a sprinkled
  order receives random kicks from the discreteness, a Lorentz-invariant diffusion of momentum (Dowker, Philpott,
  Sorkin). The order alone already blurs trajectories, without gravitons.
- **How the laboratory could test it.**
  - Classically: smooth the first arrival over a width w (an arrival time with a √T spread) and see whether the cusp
    term becomes a smooth term with the scale √(wT).
  - Quantum: a superposition of orders instead of one order (after interference and the Born rule, Q.2, Q.3).

## H4. Records live in the order between elements, not in the elements (author and Claude, 28 Nov 2026)

**Idea.** A single atom or simple element carries very little of the history it went through. Once atoms are put
together into tissues, cells, bones, layers of soil or a whole environment, their mutual arrangement, bonds,
microscopic traces and distribution in space keep a much richer imprint of past events (the author). In causal
physics this would be a property of the order itself: the record of the past is held by the relations among many
elements, and its capacity grows faster than the number of elements that carry it. Matter, "a persistent flow with a
memory of direction" (the laboratory's ontology), would then be a structure whose order keeps such an imprint. A
measurement, "a causal act" (the author), would be the moment a relation is copied into a structure rich enough to
keep it.

**What is known.**
- **An element alone.** In a causal set an element is defined only by its relations. Its past is the set of elements
  below it, but locally it is joined only to its links (immediate predecessors). Without the order, N elements carry
  nothing but their number.
- **The order holds the information.** Among N elements of a region, the number of related pairs grows as f N², with f
  the ordering fraction that the dimension estimator reads (Myrheim–Meyer; the laboratory's slanted instrument). The
  room for an imprint grows faster than the matter that carries it.
- **The age term is a record of this kind.** The static response of a field depends on its age T only through causal
  intervals of length ~T and proper time of a few ℓ_k, the needles (f68f_dev; H3). The field keeps its history in a
  non-local structure that spans the whole age, not at any point. At cusps of the first arrival (f68i_dev §7), the
  channels of that record (the rays) merge and tie, and the classical record stops being unique there (H3).
- **The quantum side.** In decoherence theory, an objective classical fact is one that the environment keeps in many
  redundant copies (Zurek's quantum Darwinism). One atom cannot keep it; a macroscopic environment can. This is the
  same statement read from the quantum side; the laboratory has no quantum layer yet.

**What would test it.**
1. **The record capacity of a sprinkled order.**
   - Put a known perturbation in the past of a sprinkling: a density bump, a defect passing, a field of given age.
   - Ask how much of it can be read from the order restricted to a later region of N elements (mutual information
     between the perturbation's parameter and the restricted order, or the error of the best estimator).
   - Compare regions of the same N but different shape: an Alexandrov interval, a thin slab, an antichain.
   - The hypothesis predicts a capacity growing faster than N for connected regions (intervals) and much slower for
     an antichain.
2. **The age term as a readout.**
   - The needle volumes read the age T of a field.
   - The question: what is the least N (needle length and thickness) from which T can be read to a given accuracy?
   - This connects H4 with H1–H3.
3. **The quantum layer.** If the laboratory gets one (amplitudes over orders), the redundancy of records (how many
   disjoint sub-orders each carry the same fact) would be the measure of how classical a fact is. That would be the
   place to compare with quantum Darwinism.

## H5. The substrate is the vacuum; its resistance is mass and polarisation (author, 28 Nov 2026)

**Idea (the author).** The substrate of causal physics is the vacuum. At the most basic level of reality it is a
"resistance" to causality, to light. At the subatomic level it is a resistance to electromagnetism.

**What is known.**
- **The vacuum as a medium.** Even classical electrodynamics gives empty space the properties of a medium: c = 1/√(ε₀μ₀),
  and free space has an impedance of about 377 Ω.
- **Polarisation of the vacuum.** In quantum electrodynamics the vacuum polarises around a charge (virtual pairs) and
  screens it. The coupling therefore runs with the scale (α ≈ 1/137 at long distances, ≈ 1/128 near 90 GeV): a
  resistance to electromagnetism that depends on the distance, a scaler in the sense of H3.
- **Mass as a resistance per element (Q.1).** In the sum-over-chains propagator, every intermediate element of the
  order carries the amplitude b = −m²/ρ. Without mass the signal passes the elements without resistance; with mass,
  each step of the order turns the amplitude. Q.1 (PASSED, replicated) shows that one sprinkled order carries this
  propagator.
- **The vacuum from the order alone (Q.1c, in preparation).** The Sorkin–Johnston state is built only from the
  retarded and advanced propagators of the order, with no external choice of vacuum. If one order carries it, the
  substrate carries not only propagation but the ground state of the field: the substrate is the vacuum in an
  operational sense.
- **The first version of the theory (February 2026):** "histories with higher accumulated resistance carry lower
  transition weight". The sum over chains is that statement with amplitudes instead of probabilities.

**What would test it (step by step, the author: "testujme postupně").**
1. **Q.1c.** Does one order carry the vacuum, the SJ state of the same region? (In preparation; the first step.)
2. **Interference (A, Q.2) and the Born rule (Q.3).** These come first, so that the quantum instrument is complete.
3. **Polarisation of the order's vacuum.**
   - Put a source (a charge-like coupling, a defect) into the order and measure the change of the SJ state around it
     as a function of the distance.
   - Does an effective coupling run with the scale, as the screening of a charge does?
   - The null model: the same source in the non-negative flow, which has no vacuum to polarise.
