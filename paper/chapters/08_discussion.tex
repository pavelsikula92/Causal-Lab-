\section{What has been shown}\label{sec:shown}

Starting from events and their order, Causal Physics reaches the following, each step tested or derived:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A substrate with a rest frame is not supported; the adopted Lorentz-invariant causal set is, and its
  geometry can be read blindly from order and number.
\item Matter is a flow that carries its four-velocity; localisation requires this memory, and the memory is
  inertia. The flows have a conserved energy--momentum tensor. Matter is also a defect of a field of the order:
  in 1+1 and 2+1 its inertial mass equals its rest energy, it swerves with the shot noise of the order with no
  free constant (1+1), and it falls as its continuum body.
\item The order responds to matter, and the way it responds fixes $\gamma$: a fixed order gives
  $\gamma=-1$, narrowing cones give $\gamma=1/d_s$, conservation of throughput gives $\gamma=2/(d_s-1)$,
  which is $1$ in three spatial dimensions.
\item In three spatial dimensions the throughput principle is the coupling of linearised general relativity
  to $T_{ab}$, and a substrate rule realising it makes pressure and motion gravitate and tilts the cones (a static response; dynamical waves and the frame
  dragging of a rotating source are not computed).
\item That rule is not an independent postulate: with the proper time of a flow counted by the order, it is
  the linear response of an order whose action has the Einstein--Hilbert stiffness.
\item The discrete Benincasa--Dowker action has that stiffness: $6.0003$ against $6$ in the static
  conformal sector, $7.983$ against $8$ in the mixed sector and $2.002$ against $2$ in the optical
  sector, computed with nothing from general relativity put in; $\gamma=1$ follows from it. The discreteness
  adds a non-analytic correction and non-local terms that grow with the age of the field, and beyond the
  leading order the sectors differ.
\item A matter action that depends only on the order sources the order through $T_{ab}$, times form factors that
  tend to one (2D; both static sectors of 3+1), and the flat order has no volume term (2D). Together the two
  expected actions give the linear field equation of the order: Einstein's, with a non-local spin-0 form factor
  counted from intervals, for young static fields. At finite $k\ell_k$ the field of dust is weaker and $\gamma>1$,
  and a static domain wall repels.
\item A history continued as a longest chain of the order falls with the Newtonian acceleration in 2+1,
  without a bending rule (replicated); in 3+1 the same rule does not fall.
\item A self-bound bundle held by its own field was not obtained. In the tested rerouting, reducing the
  capacity of events redistributes the flow more than removing the corresponding paths. In a model extended by two assumptions (a carried orientation and its
  alignment), a topological defect placed by hand is locked and keeps a cost. The defect of a field (item 2)
  behaves as a particle in 1+1 and 2+1, but its potential is chosen.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Expected values, not dynamics.} The action of the order is evaluated as an expectation over
  sprinklings of a given geometry. That a dynamics of the order selects such causal sets, the manifold-like
  phase, is assumed, as in causal set theory generally. It is not a minimum of the action: under local moves the
  weight $e^{-\beta S_\BD}$ leaves it above an escape point (2D, development). The field equation is therefore
  taken as the saddle of the ensemble mean in the manifold-like phase at linear order; beyond it the balance of
  action and entropy decides, and whether the entropic part changes the stiffness already at quadratic order is
  not measured.
\item \textbf{The field equation beyond first order.} The linear field equation is computed for young static
  fields ($kT=4$) and the bare ensemble action. Its second order, which fixes $\beta$, is not computed; its
  time-dependent and tensor sectors are not measured; the spin-2 form factor is read only from the optical sector
  and is not robust. The value of $G$ is not derived.
\item \textbf{Old fields.} The age term of the optical sector grows as $(kT)^5$ at second order in the field and
  faster still at finite amplitude; at $\varepsilon=b\,(kT)^2=\pi^2$ the field focuses light within the causal
  intervals and the second-order form ends. Every long-lived field lies beyond this point, so the static
  response of old fields, and whether they have one, is not known. Beyond the leading order the sectors are not
  in covariant proportion; whether this comes from the age cut or from the action is open.
\item \textbf{The substrate rule as an input in the tests.} The tests of the coupling (F7.1c, F7.4a) build
  the order according to the rule. They show what the rule implies, not that nature uses it; the
  derivation from the action supplies the reason.
\item \textbf{Matter as a self-bound object.} Flows are localised by their memory of direction, but no bundle
  holds itself together by its own field. The locked defect rests on two assumptions added for it (a carried
  orientation and its alignment), is placed by hand, and has a cost that grows with its size (Derrick). The defect
  of a field of the order has inertia equal to its rest energy, but its potential is chosen, it is tested in 1+1
  and 2+1 only, its active side was tested in the scalar branch, and its mass is not related to $\ell_k$. Its
  energy is conserved as a Noether charge in the mean (2D, development); whether this charge is a flow on the
  links of the order is open.
\item \textbf{The motion of matter in 3+1.} The longest-chain rule that falls in 2+1 does not fall in 3+1, and a
  field defect has no 3+1 dynamics yet: the coupling of moving matter to the gravity of the order is solved only
  in 1+1 and 2+1.
\item \textbf{The quantum.} The theory is classical. Superposition and measurement have an
  interpretation, not a dynamics.
\item \textbf{The age term.} Its coefficient is established at second order (linear in the conformal and
  mixed sectors, $(kT)^5$ in the optical one); its physical reading, what $T$ is, is not, and it may depend on how
  the past is cut. The reading, together with the optical term of old fields, decides whether short-range
  gravity tests probe $\ell_k$ now (Sec.~\ref{sec:window}).
\item \textbf{The scale $\ell_k$.} Existing bounds on non-local fields give $\ell_k\lesssim10^{-19}$\,m. Whether
  they apply to the gravitational sector depends on the matter sector, which is open.
\item \textbf{Finite numerics.} Laboratory results come from finite regions and finite numbers of
  sprinklings, with the limits stated in each test.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}\label{sec:outlook}

The road from here to predictions is concrete: the static response of old fields in the optical sector,
beyond the first conjugate point $\varepsilon=\pi^2$, then the static field of a point mass from the three coefficients, compared with short-range gravity data,
and the physical meaning of the age $T$. The field equation of the order needs its second order ($\beta$) and the
entropic correction to its stiffness; with them the completion by general relativity, now adopted, could be derived
from the balance of action and entropy of the order, or along the thermodynamic route. In the matter sector the
next steps are a field defect in 3+1 with a fall rule that works there, the conservation of its energy as a frozen
test and as a flow on links, the annihilation of a defect with its anti-defect, and a potential that is not chosen.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Gravity in Causal Physics is the response of a causal order to the matter that flows through it. The
order slows its clocks, narrows its cones and adds events so that its throughput is kept, and in three
spatial dimensions this is exactly the coupling of general relativity. The reason is the action of the
order itself: counting the intervals of a causal set gives the Einstein--Hilbert stiffness, and the
counting also says where it stops being exact. Matter that is a field of the same order sources it through its
energy--momentum tensor, and the two actions together give Einstein's field equation at first order, with a
correction at the scale of discreteness. That is where the predictions of the theory are.
