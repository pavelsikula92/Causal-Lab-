\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Causal Physics takes the causal order of events, not spacetime geometry, as the primitive of physics.
We formulate it so that every step can be confronted with a pre-registered numerical test, and we
report the outcome of these tests, positive and negative.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{Substrate.} The tests decide the nature of the substrate. A discrete substrate with a
finite number of neighbours per event and a rest frame is not supported; we adopt a Lorentz-invariant
causal set, a Poisson sprinkling in which geometry is read from order and number, which the tests support.
Matter is a flow of events that carries its own four-velocity, and the action of a flow is its proper
time as counted by the order. Continued as a longest chain of the order, such a history falls with the Newtonian
acceleration in 2+1 dimensions (not yet in 3+1). A bundle held by its own field was not obtained. Matter as a
defect of a field on all events, evolved by the retarded Green function of the order, keeps its identity and its
Lorentz contraction, and its inertial mass equals its rest energy, to $0.8\,\%$ in 1+1 ($0.9988\pm0.0077$) and
within $5\,\%$ in 2+1 ($0.978$, $0.952$). In 1+1 it wanders in momentum with the shot noise of the order, with no
free constant, and it falls as its continuum body in 1+1 and on a curved 2+1 order. The potential is chosen, and
no 3+1 defect dynamics exists yet.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{Gravity as the response of the order.} Near matter the order changes: clocks slow down, causal
cones narrow and events become denser. A fixed order gives the scalar (Nordstr\"om) class
$\gamma=-1$, cones that only narrow give $\gamma=1/d_s$, and an order that conserves its causal
throughput gives $\gamma=2/(d_s-1)$, which is $\gamma=1$ exactly in three spatial dimensions. In
3+1 dimensions this throughput principle is the linearised coupling of general relativity to the full
energy--momentum tensor; on 3+1 sprinklings the throughput principle reads $\gamma=1.000\pm0.045$, formally
undecided because the control is read less precisely. A substrate rule in which every flow narrows the cone in its
own rest frame and capacity deficits add reproduces this coupling in the static response of 3+1 sprinklings to
moving flows: two counter-streaming
beams gravitate with the predicted active mass ($2.40\pm0.05$ against $2.36$, alternatives $1.56$
and below), the cones become anisotropic and a single beam tilts them.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{The rule from the action of the order.} With proper time counted by the order, the substrate
rule is the linear response of an order whose action has the Einstein--Hilbert stiffness. We compute
the expectation value of the smeared Benincasa--Dowker action of a causal set to second order in a
static perturbation, in 3+1, by deterministic quadrature with nothing from general relativity put in.
The Einstein--Hilbert stiffness emerges in the conformal sector, $6.0003$ against $6$, and in the mixed
sector, where the perturbation changes the causal order itself, $7.983$ against $8$, and in the optical
sector, where rays bend, $2.002$ against $2$. With all three coefficients $\gamma=1$ follows from the action of
the order ($0.997$ to $1.000$). The discreteness corrects the stiffness non-analytically, $1-\hat K\simeq(k\ell_k)^2[4.0\ln(1/k\ell_k)+0.9]$, and adds a
non-local term that grows with the age $T$ of the static field, in the conformal sector
$-0.10\,(k\ell_k)^3T/\ell_k$, which we predicted analytically before computing it.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{The field equation of the order.} A matter action that depends only on the order responds to the metric
as the energy--momentum tensor does, in 2D and in both static sectors of 3+1, times form factors that tend to one;
the expected BD action of flat 2D diamonds has no volume term. Stationarity of the two expected actions gives, to
first order in the field and for a young static field (age $T=4/k$), the linearised Einstein
equation with a non-local spin-0 form factor counted from intervals,
$G_{ab}+2(\eta_{ab}\Box-\partial_a\partial_b)F_0(\Box)R=8\pi G\,T^{\rm eff}_{ab}$,
$F_0\simeq-\ell_k^2[0.66\ln(1/k\ell_k)+0.35]$. At long wavelength $\gamma=1$; at $k\ell_k=0.12$ the field of dust
is $19\,\%$ weaker with $\gamma=1.12$, and a static domain wall repels. The second order, which fixes $\beta$, is
not computed.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{Predictions.} Beyond the leading order the sectors differ, and each weakens with the age $T$ of a
static field: linearly in the conformal and mixed sectors, and as $(kT)^5$ in the optical sector at second
order in the field. The second order holds only while $\varepsilon=b\,(kT)^2$ is small, $b$ the amplitude of the
optical perturbation: at finite amplitude the optical term grows faster still, by a factor $2.5$ at
$\varepsilon=4$, and at $\varepsilon=\pi^2$ the field focuses light within the causal intervals and the expansion
ends. Every long-lived field in nature lies far beyond this point, so the static response of old fields, and with
it the far-field law of a point mass, is the open problem the theory now faces. The age terms are not suppressed
like the local corrections: by the conformal term alone, a cosmological age and a non-locality scale at the level
of existing bounds, $\ell_k\sim10^{-19}$\,m, would place the breakdown of the static response at tens of
micrometres, within reach of torsion balances; the optical sector leaves this estimate conditional. Until the
second order of the field equation is computed, the theory is completed beyond the linear order by general
relativity, to be derived thermodynamically or from the balance of action and entropy; strong-field predictions of a scalar field equation are therefore not
predictions of the theory. Every result quoted here is reproducible with the open Causality
Lab~\cite{CausalityLab}, including the negative ones.
\end{abstract}
